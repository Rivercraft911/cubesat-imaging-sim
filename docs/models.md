# Orbit and camera models

## Constructed pass

Time zero is the local closest approach to the selected target. The orbit radius is the target's geocentric WGS 84 surface radius plus the configured altitude. Mean motion is `sqrt(GM / a³)`. The model chooses the retrograde inclination that gives first-order circular nodal precession `−(3/2) n J₂ (Rₑ/a)² cos(i)` equal to the mean Sun rate. The ascending or descending branch determines the direction of the pass over the target. [WGS 84 defining parameters](https://earth-info.nga.mil/?action=wgs84&dir=wgs84), [NASA GDC Orbit Primer, p. 4](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/GDC_OrbitPrimer.pdf), [NASA Earth Fact Sheet, J₂](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html)

Pass offset is the signed angular separation from the target to the radial subsatellite point, multiplied by the target's geocentric surface radius. Positive moves the spacecraft to the right of its Earth-fixed ground track. The solver rotates that offset until the target-to-spacecraft vector is perpendicular to Earth-fixed velocity at time zero. Target elevation is geometric above the target's WGS 84 tangent plane. Ground speed is the speed of the radial subsatellite point along the ellipsoid.

The requested epoch selects a UTC date. The model solves the time of closest approach so the node's mean solar time matches LTAN. Frame UTC and apparent Sun direction then advance together. UTC substitutes for UT1, and the model omits nutation, drag, periodic perturbations, refraction and spacecraft flight dynamics. The default 550 km, 18:00 LTAN ascending construction reaches Stanford at 01:45:30 UTC on October 3, 2026, with the Sun's center about 0.064° above Stanford's geometric horizon. Over the ±360 s pass, subsatellite solar elevation stays between approximately −1.68° and +1.79°; this date provides a near-terminator example, while other dates retain the physical seasonal change in illumination. This is a local pass study with a prescribed ground track. [USNO approximate solar coordinates](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/sun_approx), [USNO approximate sidereal time](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/GAST)

## Attitude and optics

ECEF has +X at the Greenwich equator and +Z north. Body +Y is the front and +Z its top. Target tracking, geocentric nadir and Sun tracking define the nominal body +Y aim. Positive look offset rotates the target or nadir aim toward image right before roll. Sun tracking keeps the nominal aim on the Sun. Target tracking projects the target's local tangent north onto the image plane as its roll reference; nadir and Sun modes project Earth's spin axis. Near the north/south grazing singularity, a smooth transition over 0.2–0.1° target elevation limits the roll-reference elevation to 0.1° and transports that frame onto the actual boresight below the horizon, where Earth still occludes the target; this prescribed attitude has no control dynamics or rate limits, and north-up does not imply a level horizon. The main camera is fixed to body +Y, with image right along +X and image down along −Z. Wide A starts with the same axes. Wide B starts at body −Y with image right along −X. Editing either wide camera applies matching optics and local angles to both; the opposite position is a 180° rotation about body +Z. Wide mounts rotate about their local image-down, image-right, then optical-forward axes for yaw, pitch and roll. Quaternions use active `[x, y, z, w]` rotations.

All cameras default to Sony IMX676 color sensors: the recommended 3536 × 3536 recording area with 2 µm pixels. The 3552 × 3552 active array is a different readout area. IMX678 remains available. All cameras use centered optics. Native sensor pixel centers start at `(0.5, 0.5)`. The radial projection maps an off-axis angle `θ` to image radius `f tan(θ)`, `f θ`, or `2 f sin(θ/2)` for rectilinear, equidistant or equisolid lenses. The sensor rectangle, lens image circle and angle cutoff jointly bound usable rays. The 190° default fisheye circle fits the 7.072 mm IMX676 recording height and gives an equidistant focal length of 2.133 mm. These settings and the main 25 mm f/4 lens are design inputs. [Sony IMX676-AACR1, ver. 1.0, p. 2](https://www.sony-semicon.com/files/62/flyer_security/IMX676-AACR1_Flyer.pdf), [Sony IMX678-AAQR1, ver. 2.0, p. 2](https://www.sony-semicon.com/files/62/flyer_security/IMX678-AAQR1_Flyer.pdf), [Sony IMX900-AMR, p. 2](https://www.sony-semicon.com/files/62/flyer_industry/IMX900-AMR_Flyer.pdf), [Raspberry Pi camera hardware](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/camera.html)

Center GSD reports separate horizontal and vertical distances between WGS 84 intersections one native pixel apart. Footprint spans are chords along center row and column, clipped at the limb. With the center in space, ground visibility is sampled around the field perimeter and center footprint metrics are unavailable. On the default main camera the center GSD is about 44 m per pixel and the footprint spans about 155.7 × 155.7 km. The entrance pupil diameter is `f/N`, and the ideal circular-pupil Airy diameter is `2.44 λ N` at `λ = 550 nm`. Jitter in pixels is the small-angle pointing standard deviation multiplied by focal length in pixels. Exposure smear is the change in projected position of the midpoint ground intercept over the configured exposure. It follows the selected attitude law and represents center motion. Sensor row timing, measured lens aberrations, terrain relief and radiometric calibration are outside these metrics.

## Image formation and transport

Global textures, a regional satellite basemap, continuous Bay satellite coverage and camera-footprint aerial exports form the imagery levels. GPU mipmaps select the sampled level within each raster; source spacing stays separate from sensor GSD. Broad views retain same-pass Sentinel imagery. Aerial detail enters only when its resolution is useful, with hysteresis around the satellite spacing; playback does not cancel every in-flight request at time-bin boundaries. Missing aerial coverage reveals the satellite layer, with an inward validity-mask taper. These dated composites retain their photographed illumination. They cannot provide terrain parallax, current weather, calibrated exposure or spatial detail beyond the source raster.

Native images use linear half-float intermediates, a separable nine-sample Gaussian approximation to the Airy core combined with jitter/extra blur, and nine samples of center-field exposure motion. The bounded filter caps sigma at 24 native pixels and smear at 256 pixels and reports either cap. It does not reproduce Airy rings, row-dependent rolling shutter, Bayer sampling or photon/read noise. Housing geometry is provisional and is not derived from the optical prescription.

Downlink resizes native frames, runs browser WebCodecs encoding, queues actual encoded chunks against an aggregate payload bit rate and delay, then decodes received chunks. Main and panorama share the target rate; ground stitching divides the panorama allocation between two eyes. Queue overflow or interruption drops chunks and suppresses dependent frames until a new keyframe. Exact timestamps pair decoded eyes. Main encoding retains the sensor aspect ratio; IMX676 at a 1080-pixel encode height is 1080 × 1080. Encoded output is explicitly limited to 8192 pixels per axis and 16,777,216 pixels per frame before probing codecs. Codec support and measured throughput describe the current browser and computer, not flight hardware; RF propagation, modulation, coding and antenna/link margins are outside this payload transport model.
