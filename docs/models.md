# Orbit and camera models

## Constructed pass

Time zero is the local closest approach to the selected target. The orbit radius is the target's geocentric WGS 84 surface radius plus the configured altitude. Mean motion is `sqrt(GM / a³)`. The model chooses the retrograde inclination that gives first-order circular nodal precession `−(3/2) n J₂ (Rₑ/a)² cos(i)` equal to the mean Sun rate. The ascending or descending branch determines the direction of the pass over the target. [WGS 84 defining parameters](https://earth-info.nga.mil/?action=wgs84&dir=wgs84), [NASA GDC Orbit Primer, p. 4](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/GDC_OrbitPrimer.pdf), [NASA Earth Fact Sheet, J₂](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html)

Pass offset is the signed angular separation from the target to the radial subsatellite point, multiplied by the target's geocentric surface radius. Positive moves the spacecraft to the right of its Earth-fixed ground track. The solver rotates that offset until the target-to-spacecraft vector is perpendicular to Earth-fixed velocity at time zero. Target elevation is geometric above the target's WGS 84 tangent plane. Ground speed is the speed of the radial subsatellite point along the ellipsoid.

The requested epoch selects a UTC date. The model solves the time of closest approach so the node's mean solar time matches LTAN. Frame UTC and apparent Sun direction then advance together. UTC substitutes for UT1, and the model omits nutation, drag, periodic perturbations, refraction and spacecraft flight dynamics. The default 550 km, 18:00 LTAN ascending construction reaches Stanford at 01:45 UTC on November 17, 2026, with the Sun about 9.8° below Stanford's horizon. This is a local pass study with a prescribed ground track. [USNO approximate solar coordinates](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/sun_approx), [USNO approximate sidereal time](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/GAST)

## Attitude and optics

ECEF has +X at the Greenwich equator and +Z north. Body +Y is the front and +Z its top. Target tracking, geocentric nadir and Sun tracking define the nominal body +Y aim. Positive look offset rotates the target or nadir aim toward image right before roll. Sun tracking keeps the nominal aim on the Sun. North projected onto the image plane provides the roll reference. The main camera is fixed to body +Y, with image right along +X and image down along −Z. Wide A starts with the same axes. Wide B starts at body −Y with image right along −X. Wide mounts rotate about their local image-down, image-right, then optical-forward axes for yaw, pitch and roll. Quaternions use active `[x, y, z, w]` rotations.

All cameras use centered optics. Native sensor pixel centers start at `(0.5, 0.5)`. The radial projection maps an off-axis angle `θ` to image radius `f tan(θ)`, `f θ`, or `2 f sin(θ/2)` for rectilinear, equidistant or equisolid lenses. The sensor rectangle, lens image circle and angle cutoff jointly bound usable rays. The 190° default fisheye circle fits the 4.32 mm IMX678 recording height and gives an equidistant focal length of 1.303 mm. These settings and the main 50 mm f/4 lens are design inputs. [Sony IMX678-AAQR1, ver. 2.0, p. 2](https://www.sony-semicon.com/files/62/flyer_security/IMX678-AAQR1_Flyer.pdf), [Sony IMX900-AMR, p. 2](https://www.sony-semicon.com/files/62/flyer_industry/IMX900-AMR_Flyer.pdf), [Raspberry Pi camera hardware](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/camera.html)

Center GSD reports separate horizontal and vertical distances between WGS 84 intersections one native pixel apart. Footprint spans are chords along center row and column, clipped at the limb. With the center in space, ground visibility is sampled around the field perimeter and center footprint metrics are unavailable. On the default main camera the center GSD is about 22 m per pixel and the footprint spans about 84.5 × 47.5 km. The entrance pupil diameter is `f/N`, and the ideal circular-pupil Airy diameter is `2.44 λ N` at `λ = 550 nm`. Jitter in pixels is the small-angle pointing standard deviation multiplied by focal length in pixels. Exposure smear is the change in projected position of the midpoint ground intercept over the configured exposure. It follows the selected attitude law and represents center motion. Sensor row timing, measured lens aberrations, terrain relief and radiometric calibration are outside these metrics.
